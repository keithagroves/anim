# Style guide: Transformers in 60 seconds

**Look: One sentence, explained.** A calm, plain-language explainer for a general audience; the series' dark slide look, one idea per beat, very little on-screen text.

**Audience:** someone curious with no technical background. No formulas, no jargon on screen beyond "transformer", "attention" and "layers".

**Visual language**
- The sentence "the cat sat because it was tired" is the hero: seven word chips in one row, shared across the middle of the film.
- `embed` pink = the numbers a word becomes; `attn` amber = attention arcs (thicker = looks harder); `pred` green = predictions; `input` blue = layers; `old` grey = the old one-word-at-a-time reader.
- One short title per beat (h2, top band); short labels only.

**Motion:** unhurried; land beats on narration cues; arcs grow from the looking word to the looked-at word; stagger similar things.

**Truth:** the mechanism is the transformer's (Vaswani et al., 2017). Percentages and the meaning map are illustrative, and labelled so.
